# Fleet Vehicle Replacement Cycle Planning

Trade cycle is a 5–7 year decision that drives 30% of cost-per-mile. Trading too early wastes residual; trading too late explodes maintenance and downtime cost.

## Three cost curves over a truck's life

A tractor's cost behavior over years:

| Year | Depreciation | Maintenance | Downtime cost | Driver appeal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High (~$28K) | Low ($3K) | Low | High |
| 2 | High ($22K) | Low ($4K) | Low | High |
| 3 | Moderate ($16K) | Moderate ($6K) | Low | Moderate-high |
| 4 | Lower ($12K) | Moderate ($9K) | Moderate | Moderate |
| 5 | Lower ($9K) | High ($12K–$16K) | Moderate | Lower |
| 6 | Plateau ($6K) | Higher ($16K–$22K) | High | Lower |
| 7 | Plateau ($4K) | High ($20K–$28K) | High | Low |

Total ownership cost is lowest at age 3–5 years for OTR tractors. Beyond year 5, maintenance and downtime accelerate.

## Total cost of ownership (TCO) per year

For a $180K tractor financed over 5 yr at 9.5%:

| Year | Finance | Maintenance | Downtime cost | Annual TCO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $42,000 | $3,500 | $1,000 | $46,500 |
| 2 | $42,000 | $4,500 | $1,500 | $48,000 |
| 3 | $42,000 | $6,500 | $2,500 | $51,000 |
| 4 | $42,000 | $9,500 | $4,500 | $56,000 |
| 5 | $42,000 | $14,000 | $7,000 | $63,000 |

After year 5 (loan paid off):

| Year | Finance | Maintenance | Downtime cost | Annual TCO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | $0 | $18,000 | $10,000 | $28,000 |
| 7 | $0 | $24,000 | $14,000 | $38,000 |
| 8 | $0 | $30,000 | $18,000 | $48,000 |

Years 6–7 actually look cheap (no payment) — until you factor in driver turnover from less-desirable trucks and downtime that breaks delivery commitments.

## The "ideal" trade cycle

| Operation profile | Trade cycle (tractor) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Premium OTR (driver-appeal critical) | 36–42 months | Drivers expect newer; residual high |
| Standard OTR | 48–60 months | Balance residual, maintenance, driver appeal |
| Regional / dedicated short-haul | 60–72 months | Lower per-day mileage extends life |
| LTL P&D / vocational | 84–120 months | Mileage low, life long |
| Owner-operator (self-employed) | 72–96 months | Maximize sunk cost; tolerate older truck |

Trailer trade cycles run longer: 8–12 years for vans, 7–10 for reefers (refrigeration unit ages faster), 10–15 for flatbed/specialty.

## Residual value timing

Tractor resale values:

| Age | % of new price (2024 market) |
|---|---|
| 1 year | 80–85% |
| 2 years | 70–78% |
| 3 years | 60–68% |
| 4 years | 50–58% |
| 5 years | 40–48% |
| 6 years | 32–40% |
| 7 years | 25–32% |
| 8+ years | 18–28% (Mexico/export market) |

Tractor trade-in values fluctuate with the freight cycle. 2022 saw +30% spike (post-COVID demand). 2023 saw -40% correction. Plan trade cycles to avoid forced sale in down market.

## When to sell

| Channel | Best for | Typical premium / discount |
|---|---|---|
| Dealer trade-in at new-truck purchase | Standard fleet | -5–10% vs auction (convenience) |
| Direct sale to owner-operator | Premium spec, low miles | +5–10% vs auction |
| Wholesale auction (Ritchie Bros, IronPlanet) | Large fleet refresh | Spot market price |
| Export (Mexico, Africa, South America) | High-mileage, older | +5–15% vs domestic for older trucks |
| Truck-stop "by-owner" sale | Single units | Variable, slow |

Carriers with 5+ unit refreshes typically trade with dealer for new-truck deal leverage. Individual sales optimize for unit price.

## Driver-appeal premium

A 2-year-old Cascadia retains a senior driver. A 7-year-old Cascadia drives senior drivers to competitors. Cost of driver turnover: $8K–$15K per driver.

If a fleet has 50 drivers and turnover gap between "new truck culture" and "old truck culture" is 15 percentage points:
- 7 fewer turnovers/year × $10K = $70K saved annually

That's a real number that offsets faster trade cycles.

## Replacement decision framework

For each truck approaching trade decision (typically year 4–5):

### Step 1 — Calculate current TCO trend

- Last 12 months maintenance cost
- Last 12 months downtime hours × hourly cost ($75–$100/hr)
- Compare to fleet average for similar age

### Step 2 — Estimate residual

- Pull comparable units from dealer/auction listings
- Adjust for miles, condition, market timing
- Account for trade-in vs cash-sale spread

### Step 3 — Project next 12-month cost

- Maintenance trend up?
- Major component near rebuild (engine, transmission, aftertreatment)?
- Likely downtime events?

### Step 4 — Replacement cost

- New tractor price (factor recent OEM increases ~5–8% annually 2023–2025)
- New tractor depreciation year 1 (~$28K)
- New tractor finance cost

### Step 5 — Compare scenarios

| Scenario | Cost year 6 |
|---|---|
| Keep old truck | Maintenance $18K + downtime $10K = $28K |
| Replace with new | Depreciation $28K + maintenance $4K + finance $42K = $74K |
| Replace with 2-yr used | Depreciation $20K + maintenance $5K + finance $32K = $57K |

But "keep old" loses the driver-appeal value, and exposes the carrier to catastrophic downtime risk.

## Capital planning

For a 50-truck fleet on 60-month cycle: ~10 tractors per year replaced. Annual capex: ~$1.8M.

Smooth annual replacement avoids capex spikes. Lumpy buying (40 trucks in year 1, 10 in years 2–4) creates renewal cliffs.

## Common mistakes

- Holding trucks too long because "the payment is gone" — ignores maintenance + downtime + driver cost
- Buying spec'd-down trucks to save up-front — eats it in resale and driver retention
- Lump-buying (50 trucks in one year) — creates massive renewal cliff
- Selling all trucks at one age without market timing — leaves residual on table
- Failing to model true TCO — making decisions on payment alone
- Trade decisions made by accounting, not operations — wrong inputs
- Not auditing maintenance cost per unit — averages mask the long-tail bad-actors
