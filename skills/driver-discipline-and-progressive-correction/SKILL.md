# Driver Discipline & Progressive Correction

A safety culture punishes willful violations but learns from human errors. This requires a clear, consistent framework. This skill covers the practical playbook.

## The framework — Progressive Correction

Progressive correction means escalating intervention proportional to severity + recurrence:

```
Level 1 — Coaching / Verbal Correction
Level 2 — Written Warning
Level 3 — Final Written Warning + Probation
Level 4 — Suspension (with or without pay)
Level 5 — Termination
```

NOT every issue starts at Level 1. Severe issues (DUI, accident-causing) can start at Level 4 or 5. Most issues should start lower + escalate if pattern emerges.

## Categorizing issues

### Coachable (start at Level 1)
- Minor speeding (1-5 mph over)
- Minor hard-braking event
- DVIR completeness issue (forgot one item)
- Missing pre-trip details
- Idle time slightly above target
- Late delivery (occasional, weather-related)

### Performance issue (start at Level 1-2)
- Frequent minor speeding
- Multiple hard-events per shift
- Pattern of incomplete DVIRs
- Repeated delivery delays
- Customer service issues

### Serious issue (start at Level 2-3)
- Driving while medical card lapsed
- Operating with HOS violation
- Falsifying ELD logs
- Texting while driving
- Customer complaint (theft, behavior)

### Severe issue (start at Level 4-5)
- Positive drug or alcohol test
- DUI / DWI
- Reckless driving conviction
- Falsifying DVIR (fabricating defects or missing real ones)
- Theft of cargo
- Operating after declared OOS

## The Coaching Conversation (Level 1)

Most effective for behavior change. Structured approach:

1. **Within 7 days** of observation
2. **Private setting**, no rush
3. **Specific incident** — date, time, observation
4. **Open question first** — "tell me what happened"
5. **Listen non-defensively**
6. **Discuss contributing factors** — fatigue? weather? deadline? equipment?
7. **Generate alternatives together** — what could have been done differently?
8. **Agree on specific change** — measurable, achievable
9. **Document the conversation** — written summary in driver file
10. **Schedule follow-up** in 30 days

## Written Warning (Level 2)

Formal documentation of the issue + expectations:

- Title: "Written Warning"
- Date + driver name
- Specific incident
- Reference to prior coaching (if applicable)
- Expected behavior going forward
- Timeline for improvement (e.g., 30 days)
- Consequences if not corrected
- Driver acknowledgment (signed) + carrier representative signature

Driver receives copy; original goes in DQF or HR file.

## Final Written Warning + Probation (Level 3)

Indicates this is the last chance before suspension:

- Title: "Final Written Warning"
- Specific issue + history
- Probationary period (typically 30-90 days)
- Specific metrics to be met
- Consequences if any further issue
- Driver acknowledgment + carrier signature

## Suspension (Level 4)

When written warnings haven't corrected behavior:

- **With pay** (rare for trucking) — investigative suspension
- **Without pay** — typical disciplinary suspension
- Duration: 1-30 days typical
- Driver may not drive during suspension
- May be required to attend training
- Return-to-work conditions specified

For trucking specifically, suspension has cost: driver loses pay, carrier loses capacity. Use carefully.

## Termination (Level 5)

When other levels haven't corrected OR severity warrants immediate action:

- Document everything before terminating
- Apply consistently — different rules for different drivers = discrimination claim
- Provide reason in writing
- Final pay + accrued PTO per state law
- Return company property (truck, ELD, fuel card, uniform)
- DOT-recordable discharge reasons noted in PSP (Pre-Employment Screening Program)

## At-will employment

Most US states are "at-will" — employer can terminate any time for any reason (or no reason), as long as not discriminatory or retaliatory.

Trucking specifics:
- Termination can't be based on protected characteristics (race, sex, age 40+, religion, disability, national origin, pregnancy)
- Cannot retaliate for whistleblowing (drug testing, HOS, safety reporting)
- Cannot terminate while in WC claim status (some states)
- Cannot terminate for union activity

Right-to-fire DOES NOT mean smart-to-fire. Best practice: document progressive discipline.

## Documenting violations

For every disciplinary action:

- **Date + time** of incident
- **Specific behavior** (not "attitude" — specifics)
- **Source** (telematics, customer report, observation, ELD data)
- **Driver's response** (their explanation)
- **Action taken** (coaching, warning, etc.)
- **Driver acknowledgment** (signed)
- **Carrier representative** (signed)
- **Filed in** DQF or HR file

Documentation that's vague or absent = lawsuit exposure.

## Willful violation vs human error

A core distinction:

### Willful violation
- Driver knew the rule + chose to break it
- Repeated behavior despite coaching
- Evidence of intent (e.g., turned off ELD)
- Falsification (logs, DVIR, drug tests)

→ Discipline progressively, terminate at the right level

### Human error
- Driver didn't know
- Made a mistake in judgment
- Acted on incomplete information
- Reasonable care exercised but bad outcome

→ Coach + train + system improvement

The distinction matters legally (wrongful termination claims) + culturally (drivers learn vs hide).

## When a driver refuses corrective action

Sometimes a driver:
- Refuses to sign written warning
- Disputes the facts
- Walks off mid-coaching
- Refuses required training

Carrier options:
1. Note the refusal in writing
2. Have a witness if conflict expected
3. Issue Final Warning + state consequences for non-compliance
4. Terminate if non-compliance continues

Don't escalate emotionally. Stay procedural. Document everything.

## Reasons NOT to terminate

Even when warranted by behavior:

- Driver in protected status (medical leave, FMLA, WC claim)
- Pattern of disparate treatment (other drivers not disciplined for same issue)
- Documentation is weak
- Termination would violate state notice/severance laws

Consult HR or legal counsel for borderline cases.

## Common discipline mistakes

1. **No coaching before written warning.** Skipping levels = wrongful termination claim.
2. **Different rules for different drivers.** Discrimination claim exposure.
3. **Termination in the heat of the moment.** Hasn't been documented.
4. **No follow-up after coaching.** Driver thinks no consequences.
5. **Skipping discipline for high-performers.** Inconsistent culture.
6. **Inconsistent levels for similar issues.** Looks like favoritism.
7. **Discipline based on hearsay.** "Someone said" — investigate first.

## Post-termination: PSP reporting

When a driver is terminated for safety-related cause, this may appear on:
- **Pre-Employment Screening Program (PSP)** — last 3 years of safety performance
- **Clearinghouse** — D&A-related terminations specifically
- **Court records** — if civil suit follows

PSP entries do affect the driver's hireability at next carrier. Make sure terminations are documented accurately.
