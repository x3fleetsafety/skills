# Wheel-End / Lubricant Inspection

Wheel-ends are a high-risk component. A wheel-off-axle event can be fatal. Inspection and maintenance per § 396.3.

## Types of wheel-end design
- **Oil bath** — wheel hub filled with oil, sealed with O-ring
- **Grease packed** — hub packed with grease, sealed with bearing dust shield
- **Oil-bath with breather** — slight ventilation to prevent pressure buildup

## What to inspect
- **Oil level** (oil-bath designs) — usually visible through fill hole or sight glass
- **Visible leaks** — oil running down rim or onto tire
- **Bearing temperature** — excessive heat indicates failure
- **Wheel play** — should be minimal
- **Wheel run-out** — wobble indicates imbalance or bearing wear

## Lubricant interval
- Manufacturer recommendation typically every 50,000-100,000 miles
- Different oils have different lifespans
- Driver should check oil level at every weekly fuel-and-inspection

## Wheel-off cases
A wheel-off-axle event is investigated as:
- Manufacturer defect
- Maintenance shortfall (bearing failure)
- Operator error (overtorque, under-torque on lug nuts)
- Overloading

## Carrier responsibilities
- Maintenance log per § 396.3
- Track wheel-end lubricant changes
- Lug nut re-torque schedule (typically 50, 200, 500 miles after wheel-off-then-on)
- Annual inspection includes wheel-end check (§ 396.17)
