# US-Mexico Cross-Border Operations

US-Mexico CMV operations have multiple legal frameworks layered on top of each other: FMCSA authority, customs/CBP rules, immigration/labor law, USMCA trade rules. This skill walks the key concepts.

## Three operating zones

### 1. US-Mexico Commercial Zone (CZ)

Defined strip on each side of the border (typically 20-25 miles into the US, similar into Mexico). Within the CZ:

- Mexican carriers may operate into US CZ with **OP-1(MX) Certificate of Registration** (FMCSA)
- US carriers may operate into Mexico CZ similarly
- No "long-haul" cross-border authority needed
- Specific border crossings allowed (Laredo, El Paso, Otay Mesa, etc.)

### 2. US-Mexico Long-Haul Program

A US carrier wanting to haul beyond the CZ into Mexico interior OR a Mexican carrier wanting to haul beyond the US CZ into US interior needs **long-haul authority** (FMCSA's "OP-1(MX)" with long-haul provisions).

Status (as of 2026): The full reciprocal long-haul program has been intermittent — paused under various administrations + resumed. Currently: US-domiciled carriers may haul to Mexico interior with US authority. Mexico-domiciled carriers may haul into US interior with specific FMCSA-issued long-haul authority.

### 3. Through-trips

A US carrier hauling US-origin freight that originates outside Mexico, transits Mexico, and continues to US destination — still considered US interstate commerce. The Mexico transit doesn't trigger new authority.

## Driver requirements for cross-border

### Drivers crossing into Mexico (US-domiciled drivers)

- Valid US CDL
- Valid US DOT medical card
- Valid US passport
- **B-1 visa or business visa** — for Mexican entry; specific to commercial driving
- Mexican equivalent insurance card (FMCS sometimes called "Mexican auto liability")
- Carga (cargo) documentation for the load
- Cross-border driver training (recommended)

### Drivers entering US from Mexico (Mexican-domiciled drivers)

- Valid Mexican CDL (Federal license issued by SCT)
- Valid Mexican medical card
- Valid passport
- **B-1 visa** (work permit)
- Possibly TWIC (Transportation Worker Identification Credential) for hazmat
- FAST card (Free and Secure Trade) for expedited crossings
- US OP-1(MX) authority filed by the Mexican carrier

## Customs + Border Protection

When a CMV crosses the border:

1. **Pre-arrival manifest** filed electronically (ACE for entry into US, CESI for Mexico)
2. **Carrier identification** matched to load + shipper
3. **CBP officer inspection** at primary or secondary booth
4. **Customs duty** may be assessed (USMCA generally eliminates duty for qualifying goods)
5. **Permission to proceed** — release into the country

Mexican-domiciled carriers operating in US must comply with US safety standards. Inspections at the border may include level III driver-credential checks.

## USMCA (NAFTA replacement)

USMCA preserves most NAFTA cross-border trucking provisions:
- Free reciprocal cross-border movement for qualifying goods
- Rules of origin determine whether goods qualify for duty-free treatment
- Disputes via specialized tribunals

USMCA's full cross-border trucking provisions remain contested. Some US carriers have raised concerns about Mexican-domiciled carriers operating in US interior without equivalent compliance regulation.

## C-TPAT and FAST

**C-TPAT (Customs-Trade Partnership Against Terrorism)**: a voluntary US CBP program where carriers commit to enhanced security practices in exchange for:
- Reduced inspections at border
- Faster processing
- Eligibility for FAST lanes
- Lower risk profile in CBP's targeting system

C-TPAT requires:
- Security policies + risk assessment
- Personnel security (background checks)
- Container security inspection
- Procedural security
- IT security
- Annual self-assessment + periodic CBP audit

**FAST (Free and Secure Trade)** Card: A separate program for individual drivers, expedites their entry. Mexican drivers commonly get FAST cards. Cost: ~$50 over 5 years.

## Cross-border insurance specifics

Mexican operations require:
- **Mexican Federal Liability** (separate insurance from US commercial auto)
- Some US insurers offer Mexican-specific endorsements
- Cargo coverage extending into Mexico must be specified

Common pitfall: US carrier assumes US insurance covers Mexican operations. Most US policies have a "no Mexican operations" exclusion or require a specific endorsement.

## Customs brokerage

US carriers entering Mexico typically engage a **Mexican customs broker** to:
- File pre-arrival manifests
- Manage customs entries
- Handle duty payments
- Process IMPI (Industrial Property) declarations for branded goods
- Manage CESI compliance

Customs brokers charge $50-200 per crossing depending on complexity.

## Common cross-border mistakes

1. **Operating long-haul without OP-1(MX) authority.** The Mexican carrier needs FMCSA-issued long-haul authority, not just CZ.
2. **Driver crosses without B-1 visa.** B-1 specifically authorizes commercial driving on visit; tourist visas don't.
3. **Carrier assumes US insurance covers Mexican operations.** Usually doesn't.
4. **Trying to operate in interior Mexico without Mexican-domiciled carrier or partnership.** Mexican law requires Mexican operating authority for interior operations.
5. **C-TPAT lapsed or annual self-assessment overdue.** Carrier loses status; expedited processing ends.
6. **Driver hauls hazmat to/from Mexico without TWIC card.** TWIC required for some operations.
