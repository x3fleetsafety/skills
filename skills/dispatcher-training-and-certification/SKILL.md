# Dispatcher Training and Certification

A dispatcher is the operational hinge of any motor carrier. A good dispatcher generates margin; a bad one bleeds it. Most carriers have no formal training program — dispatchers learn by mistake. A structured program cuts the new-dispatcher ramp from 6 months to 6 weeks.

## What a dispatcher actually does

| Function | Time spent |
|---|---|
| Driver communication (calls, app, text) | 25% |
| Load planning / matching | 20% |
| Customer communication | 15% |
| Tender acceptance / rejection | 10% |
| Problem resolution (delays, breakdowns) | 10% |
| Documentation / TMS data entry | 10% |
| Internal coordination (safety, ops, billing) | 10% |

The role mixes operations, sales, customer service, and crisis management.

## Core competencies

| Skill | Why |
|---|---|
| Geography (US, Canada, sometimes Mexico) | Plan reasonable lanes, anticipate weather |
| HOS rules + ELD literacy | Avoid violations, respect driver availability |
| Math (CPM, deadhead %, revenue per mile) | Make rate decisions |
| Negotiation (with brokers, customers) | Capture margin |
| TMS proficiency | Speed, accuracy |
| Customer service | Retain relationships |
| Driver empathy / communication | Retain drivers |
| Crisis triage | Handle breakdowns, weather, missed deliveries |
| Documentation discipline | Audit trail + dispute support |

## Training modules (6-week program)

### Week 1 — Industry and regulatory foundation

- Trucking industry overview (asset-based, brokerage, 3PL)
- Equipment types (dry van, reefer, flatbed, tanker)
- DOT / FMCSA basics
- HOS rules (11/14/60-70, 30-min break, split sleeper)
- ELD basics
- Carrier safety policies

Deliverable: 50-question test, pass at 85%.

### Week 2 — Geography and lanes

- US Interstate map
- Major freight corridors (I-95, I-80, I-35, I-10, I-40, I-15)
- Major freight markets (LA, NY, Atlanta, Chicago, Dallas, Memphis, Florida, Texas Triangle)
- Trip planning basics (hometime, fuel stops, parking)
- Weather and seasonal patterns
- Truck-routing restrictions

Deliverable: Trip planning exercise — plan a 5-day route Chicago → Dallas → Phoenix → Sacramento → Salt Lake → home.

### Week 3 — Math and economics

- CPM calculation (variable + fixed)
- Revenue per loaded mile
- Loaded ratio
- Deadhead impact
- Fuel surcharge math
- Detention / layover / accessorial pricing
- Customer profitability

Deliverable: Rate-decision exercise — evaluate 10 broker rate offers; accept/reject/counter with reasoning.

### Week 4 — TMS and tools

- TMS daily workflow
- Load tendering, acceptance, status updates
- EDI 204/214/210/990 basics
- Load board navigation (DAT, Truckstop) for backhauls
- ELD dashboard interpretation
- Customer scorecard navigation
- Common reports (driver utilization, on-time %, tender acceptance)

Deliverable: Process 20 simulated loads in the training TMS environment.

### Week 5 — Communications and soft skills

- Driver call etiquette (clear, calm, empathetic)
- Customer call etiquette (proactive, accurate, solution-oriented)
- Broker negotiation tactics
- Conflict resolution (driver vs customer scenarios)
- Documentation for disputes
- When to escalate (safety, claims, customer disputes)

Deliverable: Role-play 5 scenarios — angry driver, late delivery customer, broker low-ball, breakdown, weather delay.

### Week 6 — Live shadow and certification

- Shadow senior dispatcher for 25–30 hours
- Take 5–10 loads independently under senior dispatcher supervision
- Final knowledge test + practical evaluation
- Certification + assignment

## Certification frameworks

### CDS (Certified Director of Safety) — NATMI
Industry standard, broader than dispatch but includes operations.

### CTP (Certified Transportation Professional) — NDTA
Logistics-focused.

### TIA (Transportation Intermediaries Association) — broker side
For brokerage dispatchers, TIA's certification covers freight brokerage best practices.

### Internal certification

Many carriers build their own:
- Pass core knowledge test
- Pass practical evaluation
- Demonstrate 4 weeks of stable performance
- Senior dispatcher sign-off

## Evaluation metrics for dispatcher performance

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Tender acceptance rate | 92%+ |
| On-time pickup % (drivers under their care) | 95%+ |
| On-time delivery % | 95%+ |
| Average revenue per loaded mile (book of business) | At or above fleet average |
| Loaded ratio (book of business) | 90%+ |
| Driver retention (drivers under their care, annual) | At or above fleet average |
| Customer NPS / scorecard | At or above fleet average |
| Documentation completeness | 100% |
| Escalations resolved without supervisor | 80%+ |

## Pay structure

| Tier | Typical pay |
|---|---|
| Entry dispatcher | $40K–$50K base |
| Mid-tier dispatcher | $50K–$65K + monthly bonus |
| Senior dispatcher | $65K–$85K + larger bonus |
| Lead / dispatch manager | $80K–$110K |
| Director of operations | $100K–$150K+ |

Bonus structures often tied to:
- Margin per truck per week (book of business)
- On-time performance
- Customer retention
- Driver retention (under their book)

## Career path

Dispatcher → Senior Dispatcher → Dispatch Manager → Operations Manager → Director of Operations → VP Operations.

Many carrier owners started as dispatchers. The role builds operational fluency end-to-end.

## Retention of dispatchers

Dispatch is high-stress (rotating shift, evening / weekend calls, driver emergencies). Burnout is common. Retention levers:

- Predictable schedule (rotating fairly, 4-on-3-off models)
- After-hours rotation (one dispatcher on-call instead of all)
- TMS tools that reduce manual data entry
- Driver app messaging vs. phone calls (offloads cognitive load)
- Clear bonus structure with predictable payouts
- Career path visibility
- Mental health awareness (EAP includes dispatchers)
- Periodic ride-along with drivers (builds empathy and respect)

## Common mistakes

- Hiring dispatchers off the street with no training, expecting 6-week productivity
- No structured curriculum — dispatcher learns by mistake (costly)
- No certification before unsupervised load handling
- Bonus tied only to volume — incentivizes high-rev / low-margin loads
- After-hours coverage by exhausted dispatcher → quality drops, driver complaints up
- Same dispatcher for all customers — concentration risk if dispatcher leaves
- No driver feedback channel on dispatcher behavior — driver retention bleeds invisibly
