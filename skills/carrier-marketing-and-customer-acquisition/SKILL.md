# Carrier Marketing & Customer Acquisition

Most trucking companies are sales-driven, not marketing-driven. But carriers wanting to grow direct shipper business need marketing capability. This skill covers the practical approaches.

## Sales channels for carriers

### Outbound prospecting
- **Cold calling** — old-school; still works for established lanes
- **LinkedIn outreach** — connect with supply chain managers
- **Trade show booths** — 5-10 events/year; targeted regional shows
- **Direct mail** — surprisingly effective; less competitive inbox space

### Inbound
- **Website** — basic info, credentials, contact form
- **SEO** — for "trucking company [region]" + specific industry terms
- **Industry directories** — listed in C.H. Robinson's carrier database, broker contact lists
- **Content marketing** — blog posts, case studies, customer testimonials

### Referrals
- **Customer referral program** — pay existing customer for new customer ($1K-$5K)
- **Driver referral** — drivers refer carriers their friends drive for
- **Industry network** — chambers of commerce, trade associations

## Building a sales process

### Lead qualification

Use a simple framework like BANT:
- **Budget** — can the shipper afford your rates?
- **Authority** — is the contact the decision-maker?
- **Need** — does the shipper have freight needs in your lane?
- **Timeline** — when do they need capacity?

### Sales pipeline
- **Prospects** — companies you've identified but not contacted
- **Cold contacts** — initial outreach, no response yet
- **Qualified leads** — initial conversation; interested
- **Demos / proposals** — sent specific pricing or capacity offer
- **Active negotiations** — back and forth on terms
- **Closed won** — contract signed
- **Closed lost** — didn't sign; document why

Use a CRM (Pipedrive, Capsule, HubSpot, Salesforce) to track each stage.

### Sales cycle expectations
- Spot freight: 1-7 days from initial conversation
- Volume contract: 30-90 days
- Dedicated capacity: 90-180 days
- Multi-year partnership: 6-18 months

## Website + brand basics

Most trucking carriers under-invest in marketing assets. Minimum:

### Website
- **Modern design** (mobile responsive)
- **Services + lanes** clearly listed
- **Equipment + capacity** (truck count, types)
- **Insurance + authority** info
- **Customer testimonials** (with named companies if possible)
- **Case studies** (specific transportation challenges solved)
- **Contact form** (prospects can reach you)
- **About us** (story, leadership, mission)

### Brand assets
- **Logo** — professional, recognizable
- **Color scheme** — consistent across truck, paperwork, web
- **Truck graphics** — visible branding on equipment
- **Office signage**
- **Driver uniforms** (optional)
- **Branded marketing collateral** (one-pagers, capability sheets)

Cost: $2K-$10K for a basic brand setup. Pays back in customer perception alone.

## Industry directories + lists

### Buyer-facing directories
- **C.H. Robinson Carrier Database** — many shippers' brokers reference this
- **DAT TrueTrak** — carrier credentials
- **Truckstop.com directories**
- **Industry trade associations** (ATA, state trucking associations)

### Carrier-buying-side databases
- **D&B Hoovers** — find shipper contact info; subscription required
- **ZoomInfo** — supply chain manager + freight director contacts
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — best for direct outreach to logistics roles

### Trade show + events
- **CSCMP** — Council of Supply Chain Management Professionals annual conference
- **NPTC** — National Private Truck Council
- **TIA** — Transportation Intermediaries Association (broker-focused)
- **Regional state trucking associations** (smaller, more carrier-friendly)

## Content marketing

For sophisticated buyers:

### Blog content
- "How shippers should evaluate carrier safety" (positions you as expert)
- "Hours of service rules every dispatcher should understand"
- Case studies from real customer experiences
- Industry trend analysis

### LinkedIn content
- Personal posts from leadership about industry topics
- Case studies anonymized
- Hiring + employer brand content

### Whitepapers + downloadable resources
- "Choosing your first dedicated carrier" — gated lead capture
- "Hazmat compliance for shippers" — for hazmat-relevant prospects

## Referral programs

Driver referrals + customer referrals both work:

### Driver referral structure
- $500-$2,000 per new hire
- Paid out: 50% at hire, 50% at 90 days
- Lifetime; drivers can refer indefinitely

### Customer referral structure
- $1,000-$5,000 per new shipper customer
- Paid out: typically after first 90 days of successful service
- Existing customer + referred company both receive thank-you

Best practice: make the program well-known. Many carriers don't communicate they have one.

## ROI of acquisition channels

| Channel | Typical Cost | Typical Result | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold calling | $20K-$50K/yr (sales rep) | 5-15 new accounts | Moderate |
| LinkedIn outreach | $5K-$15K (tools + time) | 10-25 leads/yr | High at scale |
| Trade shows | $5K-$25K per show | 5-15 leads/show | Moderate |
| SEO + Inbound | $10K-$40K/yr | 10-50 leads/yr | High; compounds |
| Customer referrals | $1K-$5K per | 3-8 new accounts | Highest |
| Direct mail | $2K-$10K per campaign | 5-15 leads/campaign | Moderate |
| Industry directories | $1K-$10K/yr listings | 5-20 leads/yr | Moderate |

Referrals are best ROI. Many carriers don't have a formal referral program.

## Account-based marketing for big customers

For top-50 shipper accounts (Walmart, Amazon, FedEx Freight, etc.):

1. **Identify target accounts** — your dream customers
2. **Multi-touch outreach** — coordinated approach across CEO, COO, VP Supply Chain, Logistics Manager
3. **Industry case studies** matching their specific industry
4. **In-person + virtual touchpoints** — trade shows + LinkedIn + email
5. **6-24 month engagement timeline** — patience

Big customers move slowly but represent transformational opportunity if you land them.

## Common marketing mistakes

1. **Hidden capabilities** — website doesn't clearly state operating lanes, equipment, insurance limits.
2. **Generic messaging** — "we're a great trucking company" — what makes you specifically valuable?
3. **No CRM tracking** — leads lost without follow-up.
4. **Inconsistent outreach** — sales rep cold calls Monday; nothing Wednesday.
5. **No content marketing** — losing to competitors who do thought leadership.
6. **Trying every channel** — not focusing on what works.
7. **Customer testimonials missing** — buyers want social proof.

## Where this fits in X3

X3 doesn't currently provide marketing tools. For carriers wanting to grow:

- **Solo to 25 trucks**: simple CRM (Pipedrive) + LinkedIn presence + customer referrals
- **25 to 100 trucks**: dedicated sales rep + content marketing + trade show presence
- **100+ trucks**: full marketing team + account-based marketing + industry partnerships

X3's role: providing the operational reliability + compliance backbone that makes a carrier marketable. Marketing without operational excellence = customers leave quickly.
