# New-Entrant Safety Audit Preparation

All new motor carriers must complete a Safety Audit within 12 months of receiving operating authority. The audit determines whether you continue operating.

## When it happens
- Within 12 months of obtaining operating authority
- FMCSA initiates contact
- Audit conducted via off-site review or on-site visit (varies)

## What's reviewed
- DQ file completeness
- HOS records
- Vehicle maintenance documentation
- D&A testing implementation
- Operating authority + insurance current
- Accident register (if applicable)
- Carrier safety policies + procedures

## 16 key areas of focus
1. Driver qualification
2. Hours of Service
3. Vehicle inspection + maintenance
4. Drug & Alcohol testing
5. Hazmat operations (if applicable)
6. Operating authority
7. Insurance
8. Driver licensing
9. Vehicle registration
10. Cargo securement
11. Accident register
12. Driver leasing/contracting
13. Specific operating restrictions
14. Driver records of duty status
15. Safety management systems
16. Safety performance history

## Common new-entrant findings
- **DQ files incomplete**: missing prior employer inquiries, annual MVRs
- **HOS not understood**: drivers operating outside hours of service
- **D&A program not in place**: missing C/TPA membership, no random testing
- **Vehicle inspections not documented**: annual inspections missed
- **Cargo securement violations**: poor tiedown practices

## Outcomes
- **Pass**: continue operating
- **Conditional rating**: corrective action required (60 days)
- **Fail**: registration revoked; cease operations
- **Operations expanded**: scope of authority can change

## Common-sense prep
- Read the FMCSA Safety Audit Guide (free online)
- Implement C/TPA membership immediately (drug + alcohol)
- Confirm all 12 DQF documents per driver
- Document HOS understanding (training records)
- Maintain accident register (even if 0 accidents)
- Verify operating authority current
- Verify insurance current (MCS-90 + other filings)
