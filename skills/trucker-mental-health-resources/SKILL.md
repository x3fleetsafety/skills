# Trucker Mental Health Resources

Truck drivers face mental health risks at rates higher than the general population. CDC data: drivers report higher levels of psychological distress, depression, and suicidal ideation than most occupations. Carriers that take mental health seriously retain drivers better and have fewer safety incidents.

## The scale of the issue

| Indicator | Trucker rate vs general population |
|---|---|
| Depression diagnosis | 13–25% (vs 8% general) |
| Suicide ideation rate | 1.5–2x general |
| Sleep disorders | 30–40% (vs ~15% general) |
| Loneliness / isolation | High — OTR drivers spend 250+ nights/year away from home |
| Substance abuse (alcohol, opioids) | Elevated, especially in cohorts > 50 |

Underlying drivers:
- Extended isolation from family
- Sedentary work + poor sleep + processed food
- Financial pressure (mileage-pay variability)
- High stakes (a single crash ends a career)
- Limited access to healthcare on the road

## Resources for drivers

### Crisis lines

| Resource | Number | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline | 988 (call or text) | 24/7 |
| SAMHSA National Helpline | 1-800-662-4357 | 24/7 free, treatment referrals |
| Veterans Crisis Line | 988 then press 1 | 24/7 |
| Crisis Text Line | Text HOME to 741741 | 24/7 |

### Trucking-specific organizations

| Organization | Focus |
|---|---|
| St. Christopher Truckers Relief Fund (truckersfund.org) | Financial assistance for ill / injured drivers |
| Trucking Solutions Group | Driver health and wellness |
| Trucker Buddy International | Driver community |
| ATRI Mental Health Resources | Research + programs |
| Truckload Carriers Association (TCA) | Wellness initiatives |

### Peer support / community

- AAA (Alcoholics Anonymous) Trucker Network — meeting finder
- Trucker support Facebook groups (mental health focused)
- Sermon-on-the-road apps for faith-based drivers

## Risk factors carriers should monitor

| Signal | Possible underlying issue |
|---|---|
| Multiple HOS violations from "missing breaks" | Sleep deprivation, depression |
| Sudden drop in driver scorecard | Disengagement, burnout |
| Repeated lane-departure / drowsy events on dashcam | Sleep apnea or fatigue |
| Missed home time (cancels resets) | Family stress, financial pressure |
| Withdrawn from dispatch chat | Social withdrawal |
| Sudden weight gain / decline | Depression or substance use |
| Repeated minor incidents (curb scuffs, mirror strikes) | Cognitive load / distraction |
| Pre-employment + post-accident drug test history | Substance use risk |

## What carriers can do

### Employee Assistance Program (EAP)

Most fleets > 100 drivers offer an EAP with:
- 3–6 free counseling sessions per year per driver
- 24/7 phone access
- Family member coverage
- Substance abuse referral

Cost: ~$1.50–$4.00 per employee per month. ROI: reduced turnover (3–6% retention lift), fewer accidents, lower workers' comp.

Vendors: Cigna Behavioral, Aetna EAP, ComPsych, LifeWorks, ESI Group.

### On-board / telematics features

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Drowsy-driver dashcam detection | Catches fatigue before crash |
| Voice-coaching alerts (gentle, not punitive) | Reduces shame, improves compliance |
| Built-in breaks / fatigue dashboard | Driver self-awareness |
| ELD with mandatory rest enforcement | Removes "push through" pressure |

### Cultural / operational practices

- Predictable home-time (e.g., 14 days out, 5 days home, never deviating)
- Driver-managed scheduling tools
- Open-door for mental health discussion without retaliation
- Health insurance includes mental-health parity (federal law)
- Discount programs for gyms, healthy food, sleep clinics
- Drug-test policy that protects driver during SAP process (reinstatement-friendly)
- Manager training to spot warning signs

### Sleep apnea screening

Up to 28% of CMV drivers have moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). FMCSA medical examiner may require an OSA screening as part of DOT physical. Treated OSA drivers:
- Lower crash rate (~40% improvement)
- Lower fatigue events
- Better retention

CPAP compliance can be a fitness-for-duty requirement (medical examiner discretion).

### Driver-facing dashcam — gentle vs adversarial

Driver-facing cameras improve safety statistically but cause driver pushback if used adversarially. Best practice:
- Use AI-only review (no human watching live)
- Coach with positive language ("Hey, AI flagged a yawn — take a 10-min break")
- Don't terminate on dashcam events alone — coach first, document second
- Driver has right-to-view their own footage

## Carrier program example

A mid-size carrier rolled out the following in 2023 and reported 18% retention improvement:
1. EAP via ComPsych ($2.50/driver/mo)
2. Mandatory mental health 30-minute training for all dispatchers
3. Driver-managed home-time scheduling (14/5/14 fixed)
4. Gym membership reimbursement ($30/mo)
5. Sleep apnea screening at hire
6. 988 sticker on every cab
7. Annual driver health-day at home terminal (free physical, eye exam, dental cleaning)

Total cost: ~$60/driver/mo. Saved $3,200/driver in turnover (industry average turnover cost: $8K–$15K).

## What drivers can do

- Use 988 — text or call, anonymous
- Establish primary care provider near home terminal
- Schedule routine annual physical (catch sleep apnea, hypertension)
- Stay connected to family via daily video call
- Find one truck-stop chain to anchor (Pilot Frequent Fueler shower) — predictable routine helps
- Cut alcohol (compounds depression, sleep disruption)
- Walk 20–30 min every day before sleeper shutdown
- Reach out to peer mental-health communities on the road

## Common mistakes (carrier side)

- Treating EAP as a checkbox; drivers don't know it exists
- Punitive culture — driver fears reporting fatigue / stress
- Mandatory home-time policy not enforced; supervisors override for "good drivers"
- No sleep apnea screening — drivers go undiagnosed for years
- Dashcam used as termination trigger — drivers disable / cover lens
- "Mental health day" announced as PR — no real budget behind it
