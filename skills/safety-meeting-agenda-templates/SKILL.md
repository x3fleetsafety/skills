# Safety Meeting Agenda Templates

Safety culture is built one meeting at a time. Carriers that hold structured, consistent safety meetings have measurably lower crash rates, fewer CSA violations, and stronger driver retention. The meeting itself isn't the magic — the consistency is.

## Why safety meetings work

- Reinforce the message that safety matters operationally (not just compliance)
- Provide a forum for two-way feedback
- Catch trends early (driver fatigue, equipment issues, route hazards)
- Build peer accountability
- Document compliance with carrier safety policy

A typical meeting cadence:

| Meeting | Frequency | Duration | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Driver tailgate / huddle | Daily (5 min) | 5 minutes | On-site drivers |
| Driver safety meeting | Weekly | 15–30 min | All drivers (rotating) |
| Safety committee | Monthly | 60 min | Safety + ops + driver reps |
| All-hands safety review | Quarterly | 90 min | All staff |
| Post-incident stand-down | As needed | 30–60 min | All drivers |

## Weekly driver safety meeting (15–30 min)

Held at the carrier's terminal or via video. Drivers participate as schedule allows; meeting recording available for asynchronous review.

### Standing agenda

1. **Welcome + last-meeting recap** (2 min)
2. **This week's incidents/near-misses** (5 min)
   - Anonymous summary unless driver opts in
   - Focus on lesson learned, not blame
3. **Safety topic of the week** (10 min)
   - Rotating: see topic library below
4. **Equipment issues** (5 min)
   - DVIR trends
   - Open maintenance items
5. **Driver feedback / Q&A** (5 min)
6. **Recognition** (3 min)
   - Safe-week badges
   - Top scorecard
   - Anniversary milestones

## Monthly safety committee meeting (60 min)

### Attendees
- Safety director (chair)
- Operations manager
- Maintenance lead
- 2–3 driver representatives (elected or rotating)
- HR rep
- Insurance broker quarterly invitee

### Standing agenda

1. **Review prior meeting action items** (10 min)
2. **CSA / DOT metrics review** (10 min)
   - Inspection results
   - Violations by BASIC
   - Crashes (count + classification)
   - Out-of-service rates
3. **Driver scorecard summary** (5 min)
4. **Open insurance claims** (5 min)
5. **Maintenance / equipment trends** (5 min)
6. **Driver feedback themes from weekly meetings** (5 min)
7. **Policy reviews / updates** (5 min)
8. **Training needs and upcoming sessions** (5 min)
9. **Action items + assignments** (10 min)

## Quarterly all-hands safety review (90 min)

### Attendees
- All operations staff
- All safety staff
- Available drivers (live or replay)
- Owner / GM

### Standing agenda

1. **Welcome + safety mission reminder** (5 min)
2. **Quarter's safety story** (15 min)
   - Crashes, incidents, near-misses
   - Driver wins (clean inspections, safe miles)
   - Trend analysis
3. **CSA / metric scorecard review** (15 min)
4. **Strategic safety initiative update** (15 min)
   - Dashcam rollout, ELD upgrade, etc.
5. **External speaker** (20 min — optional but high-value)
   - Insurance broker, FMCSA representative, attorney, mental health expert
6. **Q&A** (15 min)
7. **Awards / recognition** (5 min)

## Post-incident safety stand-down (30–60 min)

Triggered by: serious injury, fatality, major loss, OOS pattern, regulator inquiry.

### Agenda
1. **Open with care** (5 min)
   - Acknowledge the event
   - Driver wellness check (if a driver was involved)
2. **Facts only — no blame** (10 min)
   - What happened
   - What we know and don't know
   - Active investigation status
3. **What we're doing differently starting today** (10 min)
   - Immediate operational changes
   - Equipment changes
   - Process changes
4. **Driver Q&A** (15 min)
5. **Resources for affected staff** (5 min)
   - EAP, mental health support, peer support

A well-run stand-down builds trust. A poorly-run one (defensive, blame-focused) damages safety culture for years.

## Documentation

Each meeting documented:
- Date, time, duration
- Attendance roster
- Topics covered
- Action items + owners + due dates
- Distribution list (drivers who couldn't attend get notes)

Retention: 3 years minimum (Part 391/396 compliance touch).

## Common mistakes

- Skipping weeks when "nothing to talk about" — consistency is the magic
- Reading off a slide and dismissing — meeting becomes ritual without effect
- Blame-focused incident discussion — drivers stop reporting
- No driver representation in monthly safety committee
- No action items / no follow-through
- Same topic repeatedly because it's "important" — drivers tune out
- No remote-driver inclusion — terminal-based drivers get attention; OTR drivers don't
- Discussions never converted to policy updates
