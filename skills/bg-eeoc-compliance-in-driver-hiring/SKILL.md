# EEOC Compliance in Driver Hiring

EEOC enforces federal anti-discrimination laws. Driver hiring requires careful compliance to avoid claims.

## Title VII protected classes
- Race
- Color
- National origin
- Religion
- Sex (including pregnancy, gender identity, sexual orientation)

## Other federal anti-discrimination laws
- ADA — disability
- ADEA — age (40+)
- GINA — genetic information
- Pregnancy Discrimination Act
- Equal Pay Act

## When EEOC applies to your hiring
- Have 15+ employees (Title VII threshold)
- Federal contractor (lower threshold may apply)
- State law may apply at lower employee counts

## What's not discriminatory
- Required physical capability (lifting, sitting, vision)
- Required license (CDL)
- Required medical certification (federal mandate)
- Job-related background check (job-related conviction)

## What IS discriminatory
- Excluding applicants based on protected class
- Inquiring about protected class in interview
- Using protected class info to make decisions
- Applying different standards to similar applicants (unless job-justified)

## Common driver-hiring pitfalls
- **"We've been told to hire only veterans"** — protected class issue (veteran status is protected under specific federal law)
- **"We don't hire women for tanker work"** — sex discrimination
- **"Driver looks too old to be safe"** — age discrimination
- **"Driver has visible disability"** — ADA issue if disability doesn't prevent essential job functions
- **"We don't hire people from [country]"** — national origin discrimination

## What auditors look for
- Consistent application of hiring standards
- Documented job-related criteria
- Records of all applicants (including unsuccessful)
- Decision-makers trained on EEOC compliance

## Tracking
Compass HR/Hiring module:
- Maintains applicant tracking system
- Documents job-related criteria used
- Compliance training records for hiring managers
- Audit trail of hiring decisions

## When in doubt
Consult employment counsel. EEOC investigations are expensive and time-consuming. Prevention via documented, consistent policies is cheaper.
